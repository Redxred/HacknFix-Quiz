Anti-cheat

The browser-side blocks below are a deterrent, not a lock — a student who knows their way around developer tools can get past them. What they cannot get past is the database: correct answers are never sent to the browser, the clock is the server's, and every trip below is logged with a timestamp whether or not the block itself worked. Mark the log, not the block.
The student sees a warning with a running count each time. Set it high if your students sit the exam on phones, where a notification can steal focus through no fault of theirs.
No warning and no strike — the paper is submitted the moment the student leaves. Students are told this in the rules before they start. Read this before using it on phones: an incoming call or a notification can minimise the browser through no fault of the student, and they will lose their paper for it. For a phone-based exam, turn this off and let these cost a strike instead, so a student gets a warning rather than an ending. Switch away with this off and it counts toward the limit above.
Enforced in the database, not the browser. A student who is thrown offline mid-paper still gets back into the same paper with their answers intact — this only blocks a genuine second sitting.
